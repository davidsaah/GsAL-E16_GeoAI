# Course Design System

This system is intentionally provisional and is not an official USF brand specification. Official institutional requirements take precedence when provided.

## Design character

The course should feel scientific, contemporary, spatial, environmentally grounded, and confident. It should avoid generic corporate-AI imagery, decorative circuitry, cluttered dashboards, and walls of text.

## Provisional palette

| Role | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary ink | Deep blue | `#17324D` |
| Environmental anchor | Forest green | `#176B4D` |
| Spatial accent | Clear blue | `#2F80B7` |
| Warm field tone | Sand | `#F3EBDD` |
| Attention accent | Coral | `#D96C4A` |
| Background | Warm white | `#FAFAF7` |
| Body text | Charcoal | `#20262C` |

Use coral only for warnings, attention, and limited emphasis. Never rely on red and green alone to communicate categories.

## Typography

- Use Aptos, Arial, or another broadly available sans-serif family.
- Titles: 30 to 38 pt
- Primary body text: 22 to 28 pt
- Captions and source notes: 14 to 18 pt
- Avoid more than two font families.
- Use sentence case rather than title case for full-sentence headings.

## Slide rhythm

A 50-to-65-slide lecture should include:

- Opening environmental question
- Course-location slide showing where the class fits
- Short concept sequences of three to six slides
- Frequent map, image, comparison, or diagram slides
- Prediction or interpretation prompt every 8 to 12 minutes
- Explicit misconception corrections
- Recurring verification framework
- Summary built around decisions rather than vocabulary alone

## Standard slide types

- Full-bleed environmental opening
- Single-concept explanation
- Map plus interpretation prompt
- Side-by-side method comparison
- Process or decision flow
- Evidence and limitation pair
- Worked example
- Student prediction or discussion prompt
- Verification checkpoint
- Summary and transfer question

## Maps and scientific visuals

- Every map needs an interpretable title, legend, scale context, source, and date when relevant.
- Coordinate reference system, resolution, and temporal coverage belong in notes or supporting material.
- Do not stretch raster imagery or distort map aspect ratios.
- Distinguish observations, model outputs, and illustrative graphics.
- Use uncertainty displays when uncertainty affects interpretation.
- Avoid rainbow color ramps unless the scientific convention clearly requires one.

## Image sourcing order

1. Authoritative maps, imagery, and figures
2. Openly licensed environmental photography and illustrations
3. Purpose-built diagrams based on verified information
4. Generated illustrations only when they cannot be mistaken for evidence

Maintain attribution and license records for every external asset.
